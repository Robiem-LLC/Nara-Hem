Privacy policy

{{name}} keeps your family's data on your server

This policy covers the {{name}} apps for iPhone and Android, the {{name}} server and web app, the Alexa skill, and the notification and Alexa relays operated by {{publisher}}.

Where your data lives

{{name}} connects to a server that you or your household run. Your account, your household's members, locations, trips, places and alerts are stored only on that server. {{publisher}} does not run your server and has no access to it.

Encryption

Locations, trips, places and profile photos are encrypted on the device that creates them. Each record is sealed separately for every device allowed to see it and for your household's server, which keeps a copy so it can deliver records to devices approved later. Every device signs what it sends. Connections to your server use HTTPS. No one outside your household, including {{publisher}}, can read these records.

What the apps collect

  • Location, including in the background, so your household can see where you are and record trips. You choose who in the household can see it.
  • Battery level, so others can be warned before your phone runs out.
  • Your name, email address and an optional photo, shared with your household on your server.

The apps contain no advertising, no analytics and no third-party tracking. {{publisher}} never receives your location.

Notifications

To deliver notifications, your server sends a request through a relay operated by {{publisher}}, which passes it to Apple Push Notification service or Firebase Cloud Messaging. These requests contain a device push token and a generic message ("A household update is available"); they never contain locations, names or alert details, which the app fetches from your server after it wakes up. The relays keep a hash of each server's relay key, the IP address it registered from and the dates it was created and last used, for up to 90 days after last use, to prevent abuse. They do not store the notifications themselves.

Alexa

Alexa is off unless your household's administrator turns it on and links it. Then anyone talking to that Echo can ask where household members are or ring their phone. To answer, your server sends the answer (for example "Maya is at Home") back through Amazon, under Amazon's privacy policy. With the {{name}} skill from the Alexa store, requests and answers also pass through a relay operated by {{publisher}}, which forwards them between Amazon and your server without storing or logging them. The relay keeps no database: your server's address is sealed inside the sign-in tokens Amazon holds. Your administrator can unlink Alexa on your server at any time.

If you ask Alexa to let you know when someone arrives somewhere or leaves, you're asked once to let the skill set reminders. Your server then keeps that request for up to a day, and an Alexa reminder in your Amazon account stands in for it. When it happens, the reminder speaks on your Echo, for example "Maya has arrived home", so Amazon receives that sentence. To trigger it, the relay asks Amazon to wake the skill for your Alexa account, passing on only your Alexa user ID and a random ID for the request. Saying "cancel" to the skill cancels your waiting alerts.

Maps

Map images are loaded from OpenStreetMap, or from Google Maps if your server's administrator enables it. Those services receive the map area being displayed and your IP address under their own privacy policies.

Deleting your data

Your data is controlled by your household administrator, who runs your household's server and can remove your account or delete the server entirely. Deleting the app removes its keys from your phone. Contact your household administrator for any request about your data; {{publisher}} holds none of it.

Children

{{name}} is intended to be set up by adults for their own households. A household administrator is responsible for anyone they add.
